Evidence and honest limits

Safe when done right. The 2014 International Consensus on youth resistance training says well-designed, supervised strength training is safe and beneficial for young people, and that in-season strength work should replace some sport-specific sessions rather than just be added on top. British Journal of Sports Medicine

Dose. 2 to 3 non-consecutive days a week, 1 to 3 sets of 6 to 15 repetitions, technique first and light loads for beginners. Review: Strength Training in Children and Adolescents

Does it make you faster? Not proven. A systematic review of 16 swimming studies (Amaro and Morouço, Science and Sports, 2019) found 7 with no effect on swim performance. Benefits appeared mostly in older swimmers, and maximal-strength work worked best (6 to 12 weeks, 2 to 4 sessions a week). Most swimmers studied were male, so sex-specific advice is not proven. Source

Shoulders. A 2022 systematic review found exercise programs improve shoulder risk factors in swimmers (rotator cuff strength and endurance, posture, range of motion), and short out-of-water programs with 5 or fewer exercises seemed to work best. This shows risk factors improve, not that injuries are prevented. PubMed

Rest. American Academy of Pediatrics guidance: at least 1 day off a week, no more than 5 days a week in one sport, raise weekly training by no more than 10%, and take about 3 months a year off a specific sport. AAP guidance (CPCMG copy)

Girls: landing and hip block. A 2018 meta-analysis of 18 studies (American Journal of Sports Medicine) found neuromuscular training, meaning lower-body strength plus landing practice, roughly halved ACL injury risk in young female athletes. Those studies were in field and court sports, not swimming, so this block is general joint protection and not a speed claim. Source

Not proven by trials: the stroke-specific exercise picks and the sprint versus endurance emphasis are coaching practice, and each block is tagged that way. This is not medical advice. Stop if you feel sharp pain, see a clinician for ongoing shoulder or knee pain, and ask your coach to adjust around your swim volume.
